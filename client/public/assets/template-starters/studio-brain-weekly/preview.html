The deep dive: can you really “reset” your brain?

A closer look at the science behind a popular wellness trend.
Modern life is full of rewarding distractions: social media, streaming, snacks and endless notifications. When these become a big part of your day, other things can start to feel dull by comparison.

Enter the “brain reset”: the idea that stepping away from these for a while can restore your focus.

It’s a nice idea. The problem? That’s not quite how your brain works.

First, what’s actually going on?

The chemical people blame is often called the “pleasure chemical”, but it’s more closely linked to:

•  Motivation
•  Learning
•  Noticing rewards
•  Attention

Can you actually reset it?

Not really. Your brain needs it to work. What does change is what your brain learns to expect, which is why slow, deep activities can start to feel less engaging.

So what should you do instead?

Rather than a short “detox”, researchers suggest being more deliberate about where your attention goes over time:

•  Turn off notifications you don’t need
•  Make phone-free time in your day
•  Spend more time on slower things that pay off
•  Build habits around movement, people and hobbies

The takeaway: resets make good social media posts, but they’re built on a misunderstanding. If you feel overstimulated, changing your daily habits will do more than a weekend off.
