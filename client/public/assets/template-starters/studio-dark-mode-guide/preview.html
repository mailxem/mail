Designing a dark-mode palette for your brand

An often-overlooked part of accessibility is dark mode. Even if you don’t have one on your website, your readers’ email apps may switch to it anyway. Here’s how I’d plan for it.

Create a dark version of your main colours.

Start with the background and text colours. These matter most, because they decide whether your email is readable at all.
Top tip: try off-white and near-black
Pure white on pure black can be harsh. A slightly softer pair, like #FAF7F2 and #1A1A1A, is easier on the eyes.
Light background: #FAF7F2
Dark background: #1A1A1A

Choose an accent colour.

Pick one accent that works on both backgrounds. Check its contrast against each.
Warning: test your accent’s contrast
Make sure it’s readable against both your light and dark backgrounds.

Add your dark-mode styles to your email.

Put the dark versions of your colours inside a media query in the head of your email:
@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark) { /* your dark styles here */ }
Copy the example

Tell email apps you support both.

<meta name="color-scheme" content="light dark">
Quick question: what if an email app ignores my styles?
Some apps invert colours their own way. Design so your email still works if that happens.

Test the change.

Once your styles are in, test in light and dark mode on the email apps your readers use most.
In summary
•  Create a dark palette
•  Add an accent colour
•  Add dark-mode styles
•  Tell email apps you support both
•  Test on real email apps
