THE EXPLAINER · THIS WEEK’S QUESTION

Why is the sea salty but rivers aren’t?

THE SHORT ANSWER
Rivers do carry salt. They just keep moving, so it never builds up. The sea is where it ends up, and when seawater evaporates, the salt stays behind.
THE LONGER ANSWER
Rain is slightly acidic. As it runs over rock, it dissolves tiny amounts of minerals and carries them downhill. Each river brings only a little, but it has been doing this for a very long time. Water leaves the sea as vapour; the minerals have nowhere to go.
