Seals Can Follow a Fish by the Ripples It Leaves

By Wren Holloway
A seal’s whiskers are so sensitive that they can pick up the tiny swirls of water left by a fish that swam past several seconds earlier. In murky water, that’s more useful than eyesight.

Each whisker has a wavy shape that keeps it steady as the seal swims, so it only feels the trail, not its own movement.
