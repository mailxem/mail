What Does Good Work Look Like After Automation?

A hundred people who build things share their predictions
by Ada Moss
Illustration by Everyday.
Was this forwarded to you? Sign up to get it in your inbox.
When software can do more of the work, how will people spend their time? What skills will matter? What kinds of companies will we build, and where will we find meaning?

These are some of the most important questions of our time, and the usual places we look for answers don’t have them yet.

But a small group of people do have a head start: the ones already using these tools every day and applying them to real work.

So today we’re publishing Big Questions, a collection of specific, arguable predictions about work from a hundred builders and thinkers. The first twenty-five include:

•  The biggest problem will be design, by a product lead
•  Your hardest problems will solve themselves, by a founder
•  Answers will be cheap; good questions won’t, by a researcher
•  Boring infrastructure will win, by an investor

The collection is a public record of what people expect, so we can come back later and see who was right.
