# Xem brand film — scene plan v1

This video tells people building an audience that Xem makes email more human: a designed, open-source workspace for the message, the audience, the timing, and the next conversation.

The supplied Cyera film is the editing and visual reference. The reference audit contains **901 decoded frames**, inspected in full-sequence contact sheets. The plan below covers **28 contiguous beats** from frame 0 through frame 900. It is a proposal for Xem, not an already-built or pixel-verified recreation.

## Video direction

Preserve the reference's order, cut frames, framing, visual density, light/dark changes, typography switches, construction diagrams, illustrated depth, physical applications, and calm final hold. Xem identity and wording replace Cyera's. The recurring object is a mail capsule, derived from the current Xem mark's connected circles and rounded squares.

Use DM Sans and EB Garamond, with the exact website color roles in [frame.md](frame.md). Circle Carousel 4 animates templates **inside scene 13's mosaic panel**. Halftone Field is a masked light texture in scenes 11, 15, and 26. These additions are explicit departures from the source; the master layout and timing remain the reference target.

No added narration or subtitle strip is planned. The source audio remains the timing reference; its audition and the final soundtrack/mix are still pending. Keep the source's short transitions short, and its held logo still. Do not force generic video-template pacing onto this edit.

## Reading the timing

- Frame numbers are zero-based. Each scene's **end is exclusive**.
- `00:SS:FF` means minutes:seconds:frames at 30 fps.
- Output frame n maps to source frame n. JPEG filename is n + 1, padded to four digits.
- 901 frames / 30 = **30.033333 s**. The source container is 30.058667 s because audio extends slightly past picture.
- Cut frames are observed. Proposed Xem geometry, copy, and motion descriptions are not measured production transforms yet.
- A scene boundary may divide two parts of one continuing shot. Its continuity note determines whether to cut or preserve the live object.
